# Spanish (es) brief

Serves every Spanish host: the catalog is registered under the bare `es` tag,
so `es`, `es-MX`, `es-419` and `es-ES` all widen to it. Content flavour is
**neutral Latin-American Spanish**, the register of US Spanish-language
assessment and LatAm edtech.

## Register

- Controls carry the impersonal infinitive or the unstated-`usted`
  imperative typical of Spanish UI: `Cerrar`, `Buscar`, `Intentar de nuevo`.
- Sentences prefer impersonal phrasing (`No se pudo…`, `No se pueden
  restaurar…`); possessives use `tus` only where a possessive is unavoidable,
  matching informal Spanish edtech (`tus respuestas guardadas`).
- State-change announcements (a calculator opening, a tool moving or rotating)
  take the impersonal `se` form — `Se abrió la calculadora básica`,
  `Se movió hacia arriba a {position}` — never a gendered participle
  (`Calculadora básica abierta`, `Movido…`), because the participle genders the
  tool and reads as a state, not an event.
- Whole sentences authored whole; no fragment assembly.

## Gender

- Genderless enums take the unmarked masculine: `all` = Todos, `none` =
  Ninguno. Never presume the referent's gender.
- Where a form agrees with a fixed grammatical subject, it follows that
  subject: `(tachada)` agrees with `opción`, `remota`/`local` agree with `voz`.

## Vocabulary pins

- Quotes are the curly `“”`, never guillemets `«»` — LatAm practice, and the
  en-US glyphs must survive.
- The gas phase value is the noun `Gas`, like Sólido and Líquido, not the
  adjective `gaseoso`.

## Untranslated product names

PIE, PNP, TTS, SSML, WaveNet, Studio, Desmos, Polly, AWS, Google Cloud TTS,
Web Speech API, HTTP, JSON, API.

## Plurals, punctuation

- CLDR `one`/`other`, like English.
- Keep the `…` ellipsis, curly quotes and the `°` glyph; Spanish question
  punctuation does not arise in this catalog.
