# Somali (so) terminology brief

Serves every Somali host: the catalog is registered under the bare `so` tag,
so `so` and `so-SO` widen to it. Register is the neutral written Somali of
Somali-medium schooling and East-African edtech. **Assessment terminology in
Somali is only partially standardized** — the state/regional wave carries
this flag in the manifest — so this brief pins deliberate choices and lists
every judgment call under "Terminology confidence" for the native reviewer.

## Register

- Controls carry the bare imperative: `Xir` (close), `Kaydi` (save),
  `Hubi jawaabta` (check answer), `Gudbi` (submit). Somali verbs have no
  tense in the imperative and no politeness grade to pick, so no
  register-of-address decision arises.
- Sentences prefer the impersonal passive with `la`: `Lama soo gelin karin…`
  (could not be loaded), `Jawaabta waa la diiwaan geliyay` (the answer was
  recorded). Where English carries a possessive ("your saved answers"),
  Somali keeps the natural 2sg relative (`jawaabaha aad kaydisay`) — the
  calibration rule that bans pronouns globally is read per string class,
  as audited Dutch did with `je`.
- State-change announcements are events, never states: `waa la furay`
  (was opened), `waa la xiray` (was closed), `Kor loo dhaqaaqay {position}`
  (was moved up to), never a bare resultative (`Xisaabiye furan`).
- Whole sentences authored whole; no fragment assembly.
- Capitalization follows Somali orthography: sentence-initial only; a short
  label keeps its initial capital; never capitalize nouns mid-string.

## Script and orthography

- Latin-script Somali per the 1972 orthography, always with the
  double-letter digraphs: `dh` (retroflex), `kh` (voiceless velar fricative),
  `sh`; `c` for the pharyngeal, `x` for the voiceless pharyngeal fricative
  (`su'aal`, not `suál`; `xargag`, not `hargag`).
- Somali has no letter `p`: English loans take `b` (`browser`, written
  `browser` as an un-adapted tech loan, but `Xisaabiye`-style native
  coinages avoid p-words entirely). Loanwords keep their Somali-adapted
  shape (`sentimitir`, `isku day`).
- The `waa`/`ma` declarative pair marks affirmation/negation of the
  announcements (`Waa sax.` / `Ma aha sax.`); the b/w alternation of the
  focus particle (wuu/buu) never surfaces in this catalog because every
  announcement uses the impersonal `la` passive instead.
- Western (Arabic) digits everywhere: `{count}`, `{position}`, `{percent}`
  render as digits. Somali numerals take a **singular** noun (`labo isku
  day`), which is why every plural group's `one` and `other` categories
  carry identical wording — see Plurals.

## Vocabulary pins

- Quotes are the curly `“”`; the en-US glyphs must survive.
- Compounds are hyphenated per Somali practice (`meel-dhigashada`,
  `kuwa la mid ah` stays spaced).
- `common.author` is the authoring-mode label `Qor` (write), sitting next to
  `Hor-muujin` (preview) as a mode pair — the calibration finding that
  "author" is a mode, not a person.
- `common.enabled`/`disabled` render as toggle states `Shidan`/`Daman`
  (lit/extinguished), not participial descriptions.

## Untranslated product names

PIE, PNP, TTS, SSML, WaveNet, Studio, Desmos, Polly, AWS, Google Cloud TTS,
Web Speech API, HTTP, JSON, API, MP3, OGG, PCM, Hz; tech loans kept Latin in
dev panels (`browser`, `server`, `Profile`, `Simulation`, `assessment`,
`updateAssessment(...)`, `requiredTools`); the instrumentation record kinds
(`all`, `event`, `error`, `metric`, `user-context`, `global-attributes`) and
session-db field names (`assessmentId:`…) verbatim, like every locale.

## Terminology confidence

Judgment calls made without strong evidence, in priority order for the
native reviewer:

1. **passage = qoraal** ("a writing/text"). A Somali-medium reading passage
   may be `cutub`, `sheeko` (story) or `qoraal akhris`. Pinned `qoraal` for
   neutrality across fiction and non-fiction.
2. **protractor = xagalqaade** and **magnifier = weyneeye** are coined
   agentives (`qaado` take, `weyneey` enlarge) after the `xisaabiye`
   pattern. No settled Somali word for either instrument was found; East
   African classrooms often use the English names. If a real instrument
   name exists, replace both.
3. **calculator variants**: `Sawir` (picture/drawing) for "graphing" is a
   functional approximation; a math-education term for the graphing
   calculator may differ.
4. **coordinate plane = xargaga xiriirka** (the coordinate grid). Somali
   math-education vocabulary for the coordinate system was not found;
   `nidaamka xiriirka` (coordinate system) is the alternative.
5. **panel = xaashi** (sheet), **line reader = hagaha akhriska** (reading
   guide), **frame = xadka** (boundary). Descriptive, not established UI
   words.
6. **pause = nasasho ku meel gaar** (short rest); the announcement reads
   `Akhriska wuu nasanayaa` ("the reading is resting") — idiomatic but not
   a media-industry standard.
7. **zoom in/out = Dhoweey/Fogee** (bring near / make far). A camera-or-map
   metaphor; no verified Somali UI convention.
8. **colors**: `Yellow` and `Pink` stay as English loans (`Calaamad Yellow
   ah`) because no verified Somali color word for either was found —
   `guduud` (red), `cad` (white), `madow` (black), `cagaar` (green),
   `buluug` (blue) are attested and translated. A native pass should
   supply the real words for yellow and pink.
9. **speed noun**: `dhakhsa` is primarily adverbial ("quickly") in Somali;
   `dhakhsaha akhriska` (the reading's quickness) is a judgment call.
10. **auto = Otomatig** — naturalized loan, spelling (single t, final g) is
    the agent's rendering, not an attested form.
11. **off/enabled/disabled = Daman/Shidan/Daman** — the shid/dam (light/
    extinguish) pair; a toggle-state convention was not verified.
12. **session = fadhi** (sitting) is a semantic stretch for a technical
    session; `kulan` (meeting) is the alternative.
13. **assessment = qiimeyn** (evaluation) everywhere learner-visible;
    `imtixaan` appears only inside the developer PNP prose
    (`siyaasadda maamulka imtixanka`), where the source itself says
    test administration.

## Review pins (2026-11-04, annotation live-region keys)

Decisions the pass on the eight `tools.annotationToolbar` announcement keys
pinned so a regeneration reproduces them:

- `selectionAvailableA11y` reads `Baarka faallada qoraalka ayaa la heli karaa
  “{text}”. Riix Shift+F10 si aad u hesho qalabka faallada.` — the focus
  (`ayaa`) construction for the availability statement, `Riix` for press,
  `Shift+F10` stays Latin.
- Colour phrases reuse the English loans where no Somali word was verified:
  `waa la calaamadeeyay midab Yellow ah` / `midab Pink ah`; the verified
  words stay translated (`midab Buluug ah`, `midab Cagaar ah`).
- `underlinedA11y` is the passive of the established `khad hoos ku rid`:
  `Waa la riday khad hoosta “{text}”`; `removedA11y` uses the `laga` passive:
  `Faallada “{text}” waa laga saaray`.
- `clearedA11y` carries the passive of `nadiifi` and, per the noun-invariance
  rule above, identical `one`/`other` text: `{count} faallo ayaa la
  nadiifiyay`.
- Sentence marks follow the English shape: `… “{text}”.` keeps its periods;
  the short announcements carry none.

## Plurals, punctuation

- `Intl.PluralRules("so")` selects exactly `one` (n = 1) and `other`; both
  are carried in all six plural groups with `{count}` in each.
- **A Somali noun keeps its singular form after a numeral** (`labo isku
  day`, `saddex cod`), so the `one`/`other` categories of `triesLeft`,
  both `entriesCount` groups and the three availability announcements
  carry identical wording — that is the grammar, not a missed plural, and
  each group says so in a comment beside it.
- Keep the `…` ellipsis, curly quotes and the `°` glyph. Somali writes no
  guillemets; the decimal separator is the period.
